# Correct Architecture

The flow should look like this:

## STEP 1 — Structured Intake Collection

The system collects highly structured data first.

NOT:

> “Tell us about your child.”

Instead use:

* validated forms,
* behavioral scoring,
* milestone tracking,
* standardized observations.

---

# Intake Categories

## A. Developmental Milestones

Questions by age group:

* speech milestones
* eye contact
* pointing behavior
* motor skills
* social interaction
* emotional regulation
* sleep
* repetitive behavior

Example:

* “Does the child respond to their name?”
* “Can the child form 2-word sentences?”
* “Does the child avoid eye contact?”

These are structured yes/no/scaled questions.

---

# B. Behavioral Frequency Scoring

Use quantitative scales.

Example:
“How often does this happen?”

Scale:

* Never
* Rarely
* Sometimes
* Often
* Very Often

This allows:

* numerical scoring,
* pattern recognition,
* longitudinal tracking.

---

# C. Contextual Environment Data

Important for reducing false positives.

Questions:

* home language
* school environment
* screen time
* sleep quality
* trauma history
* hearing issues
* premature birth
* family history

This prevents:

* overflagging,
* bias,
* incorrect risk classification.

---

# D. Multi-Source Inputs

You need:

* parent observations
* teacher observations
* therapist notes
* optional pediatric records

This is critical.

Many conditions only appear in:

* school settings,
* social settings,
* or structured tasks.

---

# STEP 2 — Video Intake System

This is where most startups fail.

You cannot just:

> “Upload any video.”

You need:

# Structured Video Capture Protocols

---

# Example Video Tasks

Parents are guided through standardized recordings.

## Task 1 — Name Response

Parent calls child’s name 3 times.

AI observes:

* response latency
* eye contact
* orientation behavior

---

## Task 2 — Play Interaction

Parent attempts joint play.

AI observes:

* shared attention
* imitation
* engagement
* repetitive behavior

---

## Task 3 — Communication Sample

Child asked:

* “What is this?”
* “How are you?”
* “Can you show me?”

AI observes:

* speech clarity
* vocabulary complexity
* response timing

---

# Why Structured Tasks Matter

Without structured tasks:

* video analysis becomes unreliable,
* outputs become inconsistent,
* doctors lose trust immediately.

Structured protocols create:

* comparable datasets,
* measurable behavioral indicators,
* standardized AI interpretation.

---

# STEP 3 — AI Processing Pipeline

This should NOT be one LLM generating paragraphs.

You need layered systems.

---

# Layer 1 — Rule-Based Clinical Logic

Hardcoded validated scoring.

Examples:

* M-CHAT scoring
* Vanderbilt ADHD scoring
* DSM-aligned symptom mapping

This creates:

* medically defensible outputs,
* consistency,
* explainability.

This is your foundation.

---

# Layer 2 — Statistical Risk Engine

Combines:

* questionnaire scores
* developmental delays
* behavioral frequencies
* contextual factors

Outputs:

* confidence scores
* risk clusters
* severity indicators

Example:

| Area                 | Risk Level |
| -------------------- | ---------- |
| Social Communication | High       |
| Attention Regulation | Moderate   |
| Language Delay       | Low        |

---

# Layer 3 — Video AI Models

Computer vision + behavioral analysis.

Potential analysis:

* gaze direction
* facial engagement
* movement repetition
* response latency
* interaction reciprocity

VERY important:
This should only SUPPORT risk assessment.

Never diagnose.

---

# Layer 4 — LLM Summarization Layer

ONLY after structured analysis.

The LLM’s job:

* summarize findings,
* generate clinician-readable intake reports,
* explain findings to parents safely.

Not decide diagnoses.
